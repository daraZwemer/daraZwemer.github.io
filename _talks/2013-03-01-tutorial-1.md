Objectives
Misinformation can affect the accuracy of eyewitness testimony. Moore et al.’s (2024) metacognitive memory account predicts that contentious (i.e., causing consideration for whether something occurred) misinformation should be rejected more with metacognitive processes than less contentious misinformation. Specifically, contradictory misinformation that contrasts event details should result in more process use than additive misinformation that appends details. Further, questions that directly ask about misinformation (i.e., contend-assess questions) should result in more process use than questions that indirectly include misinformation (i.e., passive questions), and passive questions should result in more metacognitive strategy use than passive statements.
Recollection rejection involves rejecting false information by remembering something from the event that makes the false information implausible (Brainerd et al., 2003; Moore & Lampinen, 2016; Moore et al., 2018). Diagnostic monitoring involves rejecting false information because their memory of it is weak (Gallo et al., 2004; Ghetti, 2003; Ghetti & Alexander, 2004; Gomes & Brainerd, 2013). Source monitoring involves attempting to identify the origin of information in memory (Johnson et al., 1993). No previous research has assessed the impact of misinformation modality and type on self-reported usage of metacognitive processes.

Method
Participants (N = 464) viewed mock-crime slides and were presented with misinformation and control information about the slides in passive statements, passive questions, or contend-assess questions. Then, participants completed a recognition memory test with an open-ended question about why they chose their answer for each response. We coded their rejections for metacognitive processes participants used when they rejected information.

Results
We used generalized linear mixed effects models to assess the effect of modality and type on each metacognitive process. We expected the results to align with the account. Misinformation type significantly affected reported recollection rejection use, _χ²_(2) = 290.54, _p_ < .001. Contradictory misinformation increased the use of recollection rejection compared to additive misinformation, _SE_ = 1.12, _z_ = 12.31, _p_ < .001. However, modality did not significantly affect recollection rejection _χ²_(2) = 4.97, _p_ = .08. Floor effects for diagnostic monitoring and source monitoring meant that differences found in those models were small and not practically meaningful.

Conclusions
The results of recollection rejection offer partial support to the metacognitive memory account and aligned with past self-report research (Moore et al., 2016; Moore et al., 2024). We could not fully assess the account due to the floor effects. However, this research illuminated how frequently eyewitnesses reported using metacognitive processes. 

Implications
Recollection rejection may be elicited by contention though misinformation type regardless of the modality misinformation is presented in. Testing new coding schemes for self-report data and taking multi-measure approaches to capture diagnostic and source monitoring could determine their true usage rate, since these constructs may be difficult for eyewitnesses to articulate. 

Learning Objectives
Recollection rejection may be used to reject contradictory more than additive misinformation regardless of the presentation modality of misinformation. Diagnostic monitoring and source monitoring may not be used frequently by eyewitnesses to assess their memory accuracy or it may be hard for eyewitnesses to articulate the use of these strategies.
