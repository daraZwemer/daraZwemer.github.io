We examined metacognitive processes that could protect eyewitnesses from incorporating misinformation into their recall of an event. Recollection rejection involves rejecting false information by remembering something from the event that makes the false information implausible (Brainerd et al., 2003; Moore & Lampinen, 2016; Moore et al., 2018). Misinformation type significantly affected reported recollection rejection use, _χ²_(2) = 290.54, _p_ < .001. Contradictory misinformation increased the use of recollection rejection compared to additive misinformation, _SE_ = 1.12, _z_ = 12.31, _p_ < .001. However, modality did not significantly affect recollection rejection _χ²_(2) = 4.97, _p_ = .08. Our results indicated that recollection rejection may be elicited by contention though misinformation type regardless of the modality misinformation is presented in.
